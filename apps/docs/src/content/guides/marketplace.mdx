A `marketplace` order is sold by the operator and by third-party sellers at once. The built-in
`tpl-marketplace-order` flow (see [Built-in flows](/guides/templates/)) scores the order's fraud risk (a high score goes to a
Trust & safety approval, and a refusal declines the order), authorizes the card, splits the order into one
part per seller, waits for the sellers that must accept, ships and captures what was accepted, and
schedules each seller's payout net of commission. The nodes are in the [node reference](/reference/nodes/):
`commerce.split_by_seller`, `commerce.seller_accept` and `commerce.payout`.

## Sellers and first party

A seller has a commission (a fraction; `0.12` is 12%), whether it accepts orders automatically,
and whether it is active. The Commerce page lists sellers under **Marketplace sellers**. The
workspace starts with two:

| Seller | Commission | Accepts | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| `sel_brightside` (Brightside Audio) | 12% | Automatically | `HP-200` |
| `sel_kettle` (Kettle & Co) | 15% | When asked, per order | `KET-01` |

A line's seller is, in order: `lines[].sellerId` on the order input, the product's seller, else
first party. A first-party line is the operator's own: it needs no acceptance and has no payout.

## The split

`commerce.split_by_seller` records one seller order per order and seller, with that seller's
`lines` and `subtotal`. First-party lines and sellers that accept automatically start `accepted`; the others
start `pending`. The node takes `accepted` when nothing is pending and `awaiting` otherwise, and sets
`vars.sellers` and `vars.pendingSellers`. A seller that doesn't exist or is inactive fails the step
(it isn't retried), and the order rolls back. A retried step changes nothing: there is one seller order per order and
seller.

## Acceptance loop

On `awaiting`, the flow sends `commerce.notify` with `to: seller` (the sellers still to answer), then
runs a loop of at most 10 passes. Each pass waits 2 days for
`seller.responded` and records it with `commerce.seller_accept`:

- `decision: from_event` reads `vars.seller_responded`, the payload of the event:
  `{ "sellerId": "sel_kettle", "decision": "accepted", "reason": "…" }`. `decision` is `accepted`
  or `rejected`; `reason` is optional. Only a `pending` part changes, so a repeated answer is ignored.
  A payload without `sellerId` and a valid `decision` fails the step.
- `decision: expire_pending` runs when the wait times out and rejects every pending part with the
  reason `No response`.

The node's branch:

| Output | When |
|---|---|
| `pending` | Some seller has still to answer; the flow loops to wait again |
| `accepted` | No one is pending and every seller accepted |
| `partial` | No one is pending, some accepted and some rejected |
| `rejected` | No one is pending and none accepted; the order fails with "No seller accepted" and the authorization is voided |

If the loop reaches its limit with sellers still pending, the order fails with "Sellers did not
answer". After `accepted` or `partial` the flow reserves stock, ships and captures.

### Sending `seller.responded`

Any way to [deliver an event](/guides/approvals-and-events/#delivering-an-event) works. On the order
page, a `seller.responded` wait lists each pending seller with **Accepts** and **Rejects** buttons.
Through the REST API:

```bash
curl -X POST https://orderloop.app/api/v1/orders/ord_…/events \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ORDERLOOP_TOKEN" -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{ "event": "seller.responded", "payload": { "sellerId": "sel_kettle", "decision": "accepted" } }'
```

A seller's own system should use the [signed event](/guides/approvals-and-events/#signed-events)
endpoint, `POST /api/v1/webhooks/events`, with the same `event` and `payload`. Give each seller
its own [integration](/reference/rest-api/#integrations) limited to `seller.responded`, so a
seller's key can't deliver anything else.

The loop waits for one `seller.responded` per pass. A second response that arrives while the flow is
between passes (recording the first, or starting the next wait) finds no node waiting: the response
says `"delivered": false` (with status `409` on the signed endpoint). Retry it; once the next wait
opens it is delivered.

## Partial acceptance and capture

On `partial`, `commerce.seller_accept` drops the rejected sellers' lines from the order and
re-totals it (`vars.rejectedSellers` lists them): shipping stays as charged, and tax is applied again
at the rate (and on the base) the tax node used. The card was authorized for the full order, so
`commerce.capture_payment` captures the lesser of the authorization and the new order total and
releases the rest. The customer is charged only for what ships.

## Payouts

`commerce.payout` runs after the return window. For each accepted third-party seller it schedules one
payout:

- `gross`: the seller's `subtotal`.
- `refunded`: the succeeded refunds so far, shared pro rata: `refunds × gross / order total`
  (never more than `gross`).
- `commission`: `(gross − refunded) ×` the seller's commission.
- `net`: `gross − refunded − commission`.

Amounts are in the order's currency and rounded to cents. For one `HP-200` at $129 from Brightside
Audio (12%) with no refund: `gross` 129.00, `commission` 15.48, `net` 113.52. The node sets
`vars.commission` (the total) and `vars.payouts` (`[{ sellerId, net }]`), and sends the
`order.payout_scheduled` [outbound webhook](/guides/outbound-webhooks/). With no third-party seller it
schedules nothing. The order page shows the **Sellers** and **Seller payouts** ledgers.

## Rollbacks and cancels

`commerce.split_by_seller` and `commerce.payout` are compensable. A failed order's rollback cancels
the seller orders (`pending` and `accepted` become `cancelled`; a rejected one stays rejected) and
reverses `scheduled` payouts (`status` `reversed`). Cancelling an order does the same. A payout already `paid` is left alone.
