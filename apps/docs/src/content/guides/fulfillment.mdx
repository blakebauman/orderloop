An order's physical lines can ship from the warehouse, ship from a store, wait for the customer at a
store, or leave the till in a bag; its digital lines are delivered as codes. The
[`commerce.route_fulfillment`](/reference/nodes/#commerce.route_fulfillment) node decides which, and
the built-in `tpl-omnichannel-order` and `tpl-pos-sale` flows are built around it (see
[Built-in flows](/guides/templates/)).

## Locations and stock

Stock lives in one of two places:

- **Central stock** is each product's stock and reserved count: the warehouse.
- **Store stock** is held per store and SKU, with its own stock and reserved counts. A store has
  `pickup` (customers can collect there) and `ships` (it can fulfil shipped orders) flags.

Every reservation and stock movement records whether it was central or at which store. Holds,
releases and consumption move the stock they were made against, so a hold at a store never touches
the warehouse.

A product has a `kind`, `physical` or `digital`. Digital products hold no stock: reserving skips
them, and the Commerce page shows them as "Digital, no stock".

The workspace starts with two stores, both offering pickup and shipping, with coordinates: `loc_soho`
(40.7233, -74.0030) and `loc_mission` (37.7599, -122.4148). `TEE-01` has no central stock and is stocked at both stores; `MON-27` has central
stock and store stock; `EBK-101` and `GFT-25` are digital. The Commerce page's **Stores** table lists
each store's flags and, per item, its available, reserved and on-hand stock; **Inventory** is the
warehouse's, and notes how much of an item out there is still in stores. A reserved count links to
the orders holding that stock (**Records**, **Stock holds**).

An order names its store in `fulfillment`: `{ method, locationId? }`, where `method` is one of
<Values of="fulfillment-methods" />. On a flow with a routing step, the **New order** form has a
**Fulfillment** section: a **Method** (or leave it to the flow) and, for pickup and carry-out, the
**Store**, which those two need. The order header shows the method and the store once the order is routed.

## Routing

`commerce.route_fulfillment` has a `method` field: `from_order` (the default; uses
`order.fulfillment.method`, or `ship` when the order has none), `ship`, `pickup` or `carry_out`. It
only reads stock; reserving is a separate node. It takes these branches:

| Branch | Taken when |
|---|---|
| `ship` | `method` is `ship` and some combination of the warehouse and shipping stores covers every physical line: central stock if it covers them all, else the nearest shipping store that does, else a [split](#split-shipments) with each line at one place |
| `pickup` | The order's store offers pickup and has every physical line |
| `carry_out` | The order's store has every physical line |
| `digital` | The order has a digital line. It is taken in addition to the physical branch, so a mixed cart takes two |
| `unavailable` | Shipping: no warehouse or store holds the full quantity of some line (the reason is `No warehouse or store has enough <SKUs>`). For `pickup` and `carry_out`: the order has no store, the store lacks a line, or (pickup) it doesn't offer pickup. The node's summary says why |

An order of only digital lines takes `digital` alone. On a routed branch the node writes the chosen
`method` and `locationId` back onto `order.fulfillment` (for ship-from-store, the store it picked) and
sets `vars.fulfillmentLocation` to the store's name (the sources' names joined with ` and ` on a split), or `null` for central stock. Later reserve, hand-over and
notification steps read them.

## Buy online, pick up in store

On `tpl-omnichannel-order`, the `pickup` branch is:

1. **Hold at store**: `commerce.reserve_inventory` holds the lines at `order.fulfillment.locationId`. A
   line the store can't cover takes `backorder`, and the order fails with its payment voided.
2. **Ready for pickup**: [`commerce.pickup_ready`](/reference/nodes/#commerce.pickup_ready) stages the
   hold as a shipment with carrier `pickup`, status `ready_for_pickup`, and the six-character
   pickup code as its tracking number. The code is `vars.pickupCode`.
3. **Pickup ready**: a notification to the customer with the store (`vars.fulfillmentLocation`) and
   the code.
4. **Customer collects**: waits for the `pickup.collected` event, with a 5-day timeout.
5. **Hand over**: [`commerce.handover`](/reference/nodes/#commerce.handover) marks the staged pickup
   `collected` and consumes the held store stock.

Send `pickup.collected` when the customer has the goods, the way any
[wait event](/guides/approvals-and-events/#waiting-for-events) is sent: the **Mark as collected** button on the order
page or the Commerce page, `POST /api/v1/orders/:id/events` with `{ "event": "pickup.collected" }`, or a
[signed event](/guides/approvals-and-events/#signed-events) from the store's system. The payload is
optional.

If nobody collects within the timeout, the wait takes `timeout` to the failed end **Not collected**.
A failed end rolls back, so the store's hold is released, the staged pickup is cancelled and the
payment authorization is voided. The end is `failed` rather than `cancelled` for that reason: only a
failure runs the rollbacks.

## Ship from store

`method: ship` prefers the warehouse, then the shipping stores (`ships` set) nearest the customer. The
sources are tried in that order, and the first one that holds every physical line ships the whole
order. A store winner is written to `order.fulfillment.locationId`; the ship branch then reserves at
that store and the carrier label is created as usual, and the store's stock, not the warehouse's, is
consumed.

### Nearest store

The ship-to comes from `meta.shipTo` when it is an address object (a plain string is ignored). Stores
rank by:

1. **Distance**, when the ship-to has `latitude` and `longitude` and the store has coordinates:
   great-circle distance. Stores without coordinates go after those with.
2. **Postal code**, otherwise: the longest shared prefix with the ship-to's `postalCode`, then the
   smallest numeric difference.

Ties, and orders with no ship-to, keep store id order. Send coordinates on the address with the order
(see [Inbound orders](/guides/inbound-orders/#payload)).

### Split shipments

When no single source holds every line, each line goes to the first source (in the same order) that
holds its full quantity. A line is never split across sources, and two lines of one SKU don't count the
same units twice. If a line fits nowhere the branch is `unavailable`, naming the short SKUs.

A split writes `locationId` on each line that ships from a store (`order.lines[].locationId`); a line
shipping from the warehouse has none. The summary names every source (`Ship from Warehouse and Mission
store`) and `vars.fulfillmentLocation` is the same names joined. Then:

- `commerce.reserve_inventory` holds each line at its own location.
- `commerce.ship` books one shipment per source location. The first has the usual
  shipment id, the others add `_2`, `_3`, and so on. The output has `shipments`
  (`[{ shipmentId, tracking, locationId }]`), `vars.tracking` is every tracking number joined with
  `, `, and the summary reads `2 UPS shipments: …`. Its rollback cancels all of them.

In workspaces with the demo data loaded, the **Ship from store, with an e-book** scenario ships to New York with coordinates, so the tees,
missing from the warehouse, ship from the nearest store, SoHo. **Split shipment, nearest store** sends
eight monitors and three tees to San Francisco: the monitors ship from the warehouse and the tees from
the Mission store.

## Carry-out and point of sale

`carry_out` is a sale where the customer leaves with the goods. The order's store must have every
physical line (pickup isn't required). `tpl-pos-sale` is the flow for it: take payment with
`card_present`, route `carry_out` at the till's store, reserve from the store's stock, hand over
(which records a shipment with carrier `carry_out`, status `collected`, and consumes the
stock), capture, and email a receipt only when `order.contact.email` is set.

Till sales arrive on the `pos` channel. The default order routes send `b2c:pos` and `d2c:pos` to
`tpl-pos-sale`; see [Inbound orders](/guides/inbound-orders/#routing). The payment methods
a flow can authorize with include `card_present`, `wallet`, `gift_card` and `cash` besides
`card`, `ach` and `terms`.

`tpl-omnichannel-order` doesn't sell at a till: its `carry_out` branch ends in **Cannot fulfil**.

## Digital delivery

[`commerce.deliver_digital`](/reference/nodes/#commerce.deliver_digital) issues one code per unit of
each digital line (a licence key, download or gift card code), as
`<SKU>-XXXX-XXXX`, status `issued`. The codes are `vars.entitlements` as `[{ sku, code }]`, so a
notification can include them. A retried step issues nothing twice.

On the omnichannel flow the digital branch runs in parallel with the physical one, and **Capture
payment** joins every branch the order took: a mixed cart is captured once the shipment (or pickup
hand-over) and the codes are all done. On the till flow, the digital branch joins capture directly.

## Shipping rates

[`commerce.shipping_rate`](/reference/nodes/#commerce.shipping_rate) charges shipping. It sits
between pricing and tax on `tpl-storefront-order`, `tpl-marketplace-order`,
`tpl-omnichannel-order` and `tpl-subscription`. Its `rates` field is JSON:

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `services` | At least one `{ id, title, carrier, base, perItem?, minDays, maxDays }`. A service costs `base + perItem × units`, where units are the quantities of the physical lines |
| `liveCarriers` | Optional `{ id, carrier, url }` endpoints (https only) that quote rates themselves; see below |
| `intlMultiplier` | Multiplies every rate when the ship-to country is not domestic (default `1`) |
| `domesticCountries` | Two-letter country codes (default `["US"]`). The country comes from `order.meta.shipTo.country` (`USA`, `United Kingdom` and similar are normalised); an order without one is domestic |
| `freeThreshold` | Once the subtotal reaches it, the cheapest option is free |

Amounts in `rates`, like list prices, are in USD; the node converts them at the order's rate. The
config is pinned with the flow version, so every order on a version is quoted the same rates.

The node rates every option, sorts them cheapest first, applies the free threshold, and picks one by
its `select` field:

| `select` | Charges |
|---|---|
| `from_order` (default) | The option whose `id` is `order.shippingOption`, given when the order starts. Without one, the cheapest |
| `cheapest` | The cheapest option |
| `fastest` | The option with the fewest `maxDays`, then the cheaper |

It takes `ok`, writing `order.shipping`, `order.shippingOption` (the chosen id) and the totals, and
setting `vars.shippingCarrier`. It takes `unavailable` when `order.shippingOption` names an option
that isn't offered; the built-in flows end there, declined, before payment. Its output lists every
option offered (`options`), the one charged (`option`) and whether they came from live carriers or
the configured services (`source`). A pickup, carry-out or digital-only order ships free and takes
`ok` without rating anything.

[`commerce.tax`](/reference/nodes/#commerce.tax) then taxes the subtotal plus shipping; set its
`includeShipping` to `false` to tax the subtotal only. The order's `total` is
`subtotal + shipping + tax`. A [Create shipment](/reference/nodes/#commerce.ship) node with
`carrier` set to `from_order` (as on the built-in flows) books the charged option's carrier, or UPS
when no shipping-rate node ran.

### Live carriers

Each live carrier is sent a `POST` to its `url` with
`{ "itemCount": 3, "country": "DE", "subtotal": 42.5, "currency": "USD" }` (`country` is `null` when
the order has no ship-to country) and must answer within 5 seconds with:

```json
{ "options": [{ "id": "ground", "amount": 7.25, "title": "Ground", "carrier": "acme", "minDays": 2, "maxDays": 4 }] }
```

`amount` is in USD and `id` and `amount` are required; option ids become `<carrier id>_<id>`, and
options without an `id` or with a negative `amount` are dropped. If any carrier answers with options,
those are what the node offers; if none does (an error, a timeout, a non-2xx answer), it falls back to
the configured `services`. The `Authorization` header each carrier is sent is a deployment secret
your administrator sets per carrier `id`, so credentials never appear in a flow. The request is made
once, inside the node's step, so a retried or replayed run charges what was quoted.

## Undoing fulfillment

| Event | Undone |
|---|---|
| A failed order rolls back | `commerce.reserve_inventory` releases held stock (at the location it was held at); `commerce.ship` cancels every shipment it booked and, once none of the order's shipments is live, restocks what they consumed (reservation `restored`); `commerce.pickup_ready` cancels the staged pickup; `commerce.deliver_digital` revokes the order's issued codes (`status` `revoked`); `commerce.authorize_payment` voids the authorization; `commerce.invoice` takes an on-account invoice back off the customer's balance |
| A cancel | Does the same in one pass: releases holds, voids authorizations, cancels staged pickups along with open shipments, restocks shipped stock, reverses account charges, revokes codes, plus seller orders and payouts |

Codes issued for a mixed cart are revoked when its physical part later fails, for example when the
store runs out between routing and reserving. A completed hand-over has consumed its stock and has no
rollback; returns go through the return steps.

## Events

`order.ready_for_pickup` is sent when `commerce.pickup_ready` finishes. `order.delivered` is sent
when `commerce.handover` or `commerce.deliver_digital` finishes, so a mixed cart can send it twice.
See [Outbound webhooks](/guides/outbound-webhooks/).
