Each person signs in with their own email and password, and has one role. API tokens carry a role
too. The roles are ordered, so each one can do everything the roles before it can:

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| `viewer` | See orders, flows, approvals and the back office; use the [assistant](/guides/assistant/) to read |
| `approver` | Also approve and reject approvals, including through the assistant |
| `operator` | Also start, cancel and restart orders, deliver events, create, edit and publish flows, run the stock review, have the assistant do those and draft flow changes, manage webhook subscriptions, and manage the team and API tokens |

The roles and the order are `ROLES` in `packages/core/src/roles.ts`. Every route checks the role on
the server; the dashboard also hides controls a role can't use.

## Setting up sign-in

A deployment with no `APP_PASSWORD` and no people is open: everyone acts as an operator, which is
the local development default. To turn sign-in on, open **Set up sign-in** in the account menu (the Team
page) and create your operator account with a password you choose. You are signed in as it, and
from then on everyone signs in.

With `APP_PASSWORD` set, sign-in is on from the start. On the sign-in page, choose **Use the
workspace password instead** and enter it to reach the Team page as an operator. (The form sends no
email; the REST API's equivalent is the password as a bearer token.) The password stays a break-glass operator sign-in
and API credential; it has no identity, so its decisions are recorded as `operator`.

## The Team page

Operators manage people and tokens at `/team`:

- **Add person:** email, optional name and role. The page shows a generated 20-character
  [temporary password](#temporary-passwords) once, with a **Copy** button; send it to them
  privately.
- **Role:** change and save. You can't lower your own role.
- **Reset password:** after you confirm, gives the person a new temporary password and signs them
  out everywhere.
- **Disable / Enable:** after you confirm, a disabled person can't sign in, and their sessions end. You can't disable yourself.
- **Reset two-factor:** for someone who has lost their phone and their recovery codes. After you
  confirm, it turns
  their [two-factor sign-in](#two-factor-sign-in) off, so they sign in with their password alone and
  can set it up again. You can't reset your own.
- Without `APP_PASSWORD`, the last active operator can't be demoted or disabled, so someone can
  always manage the team.

Passwords must be 10 to 256 characters, not a common password and not contain the email address or its part before the `@` (when that is 4 or more characters).
They are stored as PBKDF2-SHA256 (100,000 iterations, the Workers limit) as
`pbkdf2:<iterations>:<salt>:<hash>`.

A session is a `__Host-ol_session` cookie holding a random token; D1 stores only its SHA-256. It
ends 30 days after sign-in, or after 7 days without use, whichever comes first
(`SESSION_MAX_MS` and `SESSION_IDLE_MS` in `packages/core/src/roles.ts`). Pages and the order page's
live connection both count as use, recorded at most every 5 minutes. Signing out deletes the
session.

### Temporary passwords

A password an operator sets (a new person's, or a reset) is temporary. Signing in with it works,
but until the person chooses their own on the **Account** page, every other page sends them there,
and the REST API, MCP, A2A, the order page's live connection and order documents answer `403`.
Choosing a new password ends it, and the Team page marks people still on one with
"temporary password". It is `users.password_change_due` in D1.

### Your sessions

The **Account** page lists where you're signed in: each session's browser and system, address,
sign-in time and last activity, with this browser marked. **Sign out** ends one other session;
**Sign out everywhere else** ends all but this one. Changing your password also signs you out
everywhere else. An operator's **Reset password** or **Disable** on the Team page ends all of a
person's sessions.

### Two-factor sign-in

On the **Account** page, **Set up two-factor sign-in** shows a QR code and the same key as text.
Scan it with an authenticator app (1Password, Google Authenticator, Authy and others), then enter
the code the app shows and your password to turn it on. From then on, after a correct password,
sign-in goes to `/login/two-factor` and asks for the app's current 6-digit code. You have 10
minutes to enter it.

Turning it on shows 10 recovery codes once, like `k7mqp-3xw9d`, with **Copy** and **Download**;
**I've saved them** moves on. Each code signs you in once instead of an app code, for a lost or
broken phone (on the sign-in step, **Use a recovery code**); the Account page shows how many are
left and warns at three. **New recovery codes** replaces them all, and **Turn off** removes
two-factor sign-in after a confirmation; each asks for your password. If you have no phone and no
codes left, an operator can **Reset two-factor** on the Team page.

Codes are time-based one-time passwords (RFC 6238: HMAC-SHA1, 30-second steps, 6 digits). The code
for the step before or after the current one is accepted too, in case the phone's clock is a little
off, and each step's code works once. Recovery codes are stored as SHA-256 and deleted when used.
The app's secret has to be readable to check codes, so it is stored as is, in `users.totp_secret`.
The workspace password and API tokens don't use two-factor sign-in.

#### Requiring it

Operators choose who must use two-factor sign-in under **Two-factor sign-in** on the Team page:
nobody (the default), operators, approvers and operators, or everyone. The section counts the people
covered who haven't set it up. Until they do, those people can only use their Account page: every
other page sends them there, and the REST API, the order page's live connection and order documents
answer `403`. They can't turn it off while it is required, but can make new recovery codes. Changing
the setting takes effect at once on the isolate that saved it and within 30 seconds everywhere else.
It is stored in D1 `workspace_settings`. Turn it on for your own role after setting up your own
two-factor sign-in, or you'll be sent to your Account page to do it first.

### Failed sign-ins

Failed sign-ins are counted in 15-minute windows, and an attempt over a limit is refused with
`429` and a wait before any password or code is checked, even when it is right:

| Counted per | Failures allowed | Stops |
|---|---|---|
| Email and address together | 5 | Guessing one person's password from one place |
| Address | 30 | One place trying many accounts |
| Email | 50 | Many places trying one account; high, so nobody can easily lock a person out |
| Person, at the code step | 5 | Guessing two-factor codes after a correct password |

A successful sign-in clears its email-and-address count. The workspace password counts as one
email. Separately, 20 wrong guesses at the shared password as an API bearer token from one address in 15 minutes are refused with `429`. Addresses come from `cf-connecting-ip`, which Cloudflare sets. The limits are `LIMITS` in
`apps/web/app/lib/throttle.ts`, and the counts are D1 `auth_failures`.

## API tokens

Systems that call the [REST API](/reference/rest-api/) use a token:

```bash
curl https://orderloop.io/api/v1/orders -H "Authorization: Bearer ol_live_…"
```

Create one on the Team page with a name (what will use it), a role and how long it lasts: 30 days,
90 days (the default), 1 year or never. The token is shown once and stored only as a SHA-256; the
list shows its first 12 characters, who created it, when it was last used and when it expires,
highlighting tokens that expire within 14 days. Revoke it there. An expired token gets `401` with
`This API token expired on <date>…`; create a new one and switch the integration over before the
old one runs out. Tokens made before expiry existed never expire. A token's role limits it like a person's: an `approver` token can
decide approvals but not start orders. Approval decisions made with a token are recorded as
`token:<name>`.

## Connected apps

MCP clients such as Claude connect at `/mcp`, and A2A agents at `/a2a`; each asks you to approve it on a consent page, where
you sign in (two-factor included) and choose a role. Connecting needs your own account: the
workspace password belongs to no one, so it can't stand behind an app, and the consent page asks
whoever is signed in with it to sign in as themselves. You can grant your own role or a lower one,
never a higher one. **Account** lists **Connected apps**, each with whether it is an AI client
(MCP) or an agent (A2A), the address its access goes to, its role and when it connected;
**Disconnect** ends it after a confirmation. Every request from a connected app re-reads you: if you are
disabled it stops working, and its role is the lower of what you granted and what you have now. What
it does is recorded as `<email> via MCP` or `<email> via A2A`. A grant is for one endpoint, so
an app that uses both is connected twice. See [MCP server](/reference/mcp/) and
[A2A endpoint](/reference/a2a/).

## Audit log

Operators see security events at **Audit log** (in the account menu and linked from the Team page, `/audit`), newest first,
filtered by kind, and through [`GET /api/v1/audit-events`](/reference/rest-api/#audit-events).
Each event records when, who acted, what they acted on, details and the address:

| Kind | Actions |
|---|---|
| Sign-in | `auth.sign_in`, `auth.sign_in_failed` (with the email tried and why: unknown email, wrong password, disabled, no password set, wrong workspace password), `auth.sign_in_throttled`, `auth.sign_out`, `auth.password_changed`, `auth.session_ended` (device and address), `auth.other_sessions_ended` (how many), `auth.two_factor_enabled`, `auth.two_factor_disabled`, `auth.two_factor_failed` (wrong code or wrong recovery code), `auth.two_factor_throttled`, `auth.recovery_codes_replaced`, `auth.recovery_code_used` (how many are left). A two-factor `auth.sign_in` records whether it used the app or a recovery code |
| Team | `team.first_operator`, `team.user_added`, `team.role_changed` (from and to), `team.password_reset`, `team.user_disabled`, `team.user_enabled`, `team.two_factor_reset`, `team.two_factor_requirement` (from and to) |
| API tokens | `token.created` (role, prefix and `expiresAt`), `token.revoked` |
| Connected apps | `oauth.app_connected` (role and client id), `oauth.app_disconnected` |
| API access | `api.bearer_failed` and `api.bearer_throttled`, for wrong guesses at the shared password; `api.token_expired`, at most hourly per token, when an expired token is used |

A person signing in only hears that the email and password didn't match; the reason is for
operators. Passwords and tokens are never recorded. Events are kept 180 days. Writing an event
never blocks the action it records. Approval decisions are recorded on the approval itself (below),
not here.

## Who decided

An approval's `by` is the person who decided: their email for a signed-in person, `token:<name>`
for an API token. Through the [assistant](/guides/assistant/), MCP or A2A it is `<email> via assistant`, `<email> via MCP` or `<email> via A2A`. On the order page's live connection, the agent takes the name from the
connection, never from what the page sends; see
[OrderAgent](/internals/order-agent/#roles-on-the-socket).

Changes reach pages that are already open. Each action on the order page's live connection checks
the person's session again, so a lower role applies to the next action, and signing out, a
password reset, being disabled or the session going idle ends it.

## Cross-site requests

Every `*.orderloop.io` host counts as the same site, so the `SameSite=Lax` cookie would still be
sent with a form post from a sibling subdomain. The web Worker therefore refuses a browser request
that changes something (any method but `GET`, `HEAD` and `OPTIONS`), or opens the order page's
WebSocket, unless its `Origin` is the app's own; without `Origin` it reads `Sec-Fetch-Site`, and
accepts only `same-origin` or `none`. The answer is `403 { "error": "Cross-site request refused" }`.
Requests with a bearer token are exempt, since they carry no cookie, and so are clients that send
neither header. The check is `crossOrigin` in `apps/web/app/lib/origin.ts`.

## Outside identity providers

`users.external_sub` is reserved for linking an outside identity. Signing in through an OpenID
Connect provider would match a person by that subject and start the same kind of session, keeping
roles and tokens in OrderLoop.
