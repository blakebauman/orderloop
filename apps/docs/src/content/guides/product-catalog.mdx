The product catalog is where a workspace keeps what it sells in a shape a storefront can read:
products with typed attributes, variants, per-channel prices and categories. Integrators write it
through `/api/v1/catalog/*` (see the [OpenAPI explorer](/api/) for every route and schema), and a
storefront reads it from a separate, public, cached API. The catalog also keeps each variant's name and
list price current for [orders](#orders).

## The model

| Concept | What it is |
|---|---|
| Product | A `key`, a `type`, localized `title`, `slug` and `description`, a `brand`, `categories`, `media`, attribute values, `variants`, and a listing per channel. `status` is one of <Values of="product-statuses" />; only `active` products are published. `extra` is passed through to readers but never indexed |
| Product type | The shape of a product: its attributes and variant axes. Referenced as `code` (the latest version) or `code@version`; a stored product records the version it was validated against |
| Variant | One sellable SKU. `sku` is unique within the product. `gtin` is optional: a GTIN-8, UPC (12 digits), EAN (13) or GTIN-14, with a valid GS1 check digit, published as written. `mpn` is the optional manufacturer part number (up to 70 characters). A variant can carry its own attribute values, prices and media, and its per-unit shipping `weightKg` and `dimensionsCm` (`{ length, width, height }`), which order shipping can be [rated by](/guides/fulfillment/#rating-by-weight) and which aren't published. A product has 1 to 250 variants |
| Channel | A place you sell: a `code`, a `name`, one `currency`, its `locales` and a `defaultLocale` that missing translations fall back to |
| Category | A tree node: `code`, `parent` (or `null`), a localized `name`, an optional localized `slug` and a `position` |

SKUs, GTINs and slugs identify one product: a product that claims one another product holds is
rejected with `already used by <key>`. A GTIN is one identifier in every length, so a UPC and the
same item's EAN or GTIN-14 (the UPC with leading zeros) are the same claim, and either finds it.
`GET /api/v1/catalog/aliases/:alias` finds the holder of `sku:<sku>`, `gtin:<gtin>` or
`slug:<locale>:<slug>`.

An MPN is unique only within its brand, so it isn't claimed: two products may share one. Search
matches SKUs, GTINs and MPNs, so a shopper can find a product by any of them.

### Product types and versions

A type changes by adding a version, never by editing one. The next version must be the current
version plus one and may only add attributes, add enum values, relax `required`, or mark an
attribute `deprecated`. Removing an attribute or an enum value, changing a kind, scope, unit or
whether text is localized, making an attribute required, or changing the variant axes is refused
with `409 { error: "Type refused", issues }`. Existing products keep validating against the
version they name.

An attribute has a `code`, a `kind`, a `scope` (`product` or `variant`) and these flags:

| Field | Effect |
|---|---|
| `required` | A product (or variant, by scope) without a value is rejected |
| `localized` | Text only: one value per locale, written as `{ "en": "…", "de": "…" }` |
| `filterable` | Counted as facets and usable as `f.<code>` and `r.<code>` filters on category listings. Not available for `json` |
| `searchable` | Its text goes into keyword search |
| `sortable` | Offered as `sort=<code>_asc` and `_desc` on listings. Numeric kinds only |
| `card` | Included on the cards listings and batch lookups return |
| `values`, `unit`, `buckets` | The allowed values of an `enum` or `multi_enum` (with labels); the unit a `measure` is stored in; ascending edges for range facets |

Attribute kinds: <Values of="attribute-kinds" />. Listings filter and count the kinds
<Values of="term-kinds" /> as terms (not localized text), and filter by range and sort the kinds
<Values of="numeric-kinds" />. Prices are always filterable and sortable.

`variantAxes` (up to 5) name variant-scoped `enum` attributes that tell variants apart, such as size and
color. A variant must give a value for every axis, and no two variants may have the same combination.

### Prices

A price is `{ "amount": 1999, "currency": "USD" }` in minor units (cents; whole units for
zero-decimal currencies) and must be in the channel's currency, or it is ignored for that channel. A
product's listing per channel is `{ visible, price?, compareAt? }` (`compareAt` is the struck-through
"was" price); a variant's `prices.<channel>` overrides it. A product that isn't `visible` in a
channel, isn't `active`, or has no active variant is not published there.

### Categories

A product lists under every category in `categories`, and also under each of their ancestors: a
product in `tees`, a child of `tops`, appears in the listings of both. `primaryCategory` (one of
`categories`, defaulting to the first) is the breadcrumb category. If you move a category, products
already published keep their old ancestors until a [reindex](#freshness).

## Writing products

`POST /api/v1/catalog/products` takes `{ "products": [...], "urgent"?: true }` with 1 to 500 products
and answers `{ "results": [{ "key", "status", "version"?, "issues"? }] }`, one per product. `status` is:

| Result | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `created`, `updated` | Stored; `version` counts changes to the product, starting at 1 |
| `unchanged` | The content is identical to what is stored. Nothing is written or republished, so sending a full file every night is cheap |
| `rejected` | Not stored. `issues` lists each problem as a `path` and `message`; the valid products in the same call are stored |

If a key appears twice in one call, the last one wins and the earlier ones are rejected.
`POST /api/v1/catalog/products/delete` takes `{ "keys": [...], "urgent"?: true }` (1 to 500) and answers
`deleted` per key. `GET /api/v1/catalog/products/:key` returns the stored product as written.
Types, channels and categories are written with `POST /api/v1/catalog/types`, `/channels` and
`/categories` (and listed with `GET`); categories are upserted, and the tree is republished once per call.

### Deleting types, channels and categories

| Request | Needs | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `DELETE /api/v1/catalog/types/:code` | `operator` | Removes every version of the type and answers `{ ok, code, versions }`. Refused with `409` while any product uses the type (`N products use <code>: delete or move them first`) |
| `POST /api/v1/catalog/categories/delete` with `{ "codes": [...] }` (1 to 500) | `operator` | Deletes the categories, republishes each channel's tree and removes their listings, and answers `{ ok, deleted }`. Refused with `409` while a product is filed in one, or one is still the parent of a category outside the set; delete a parent and its children in one call |
| `DELETE /api/v1/catalog/channels/:code` | `admin` | Removes the channel and its category trees, then takes down everything published in it: product documents, SKU and slug lookups, listing cards, category listings and search entries. Answers `{ ok, code, unpublished }`, the number of products taken down |

An unknown code answers `404`; any other refusal is `409` with its `issues`. Deleting a channel doesn't
touch the products: they keep their listing for it, so adding the channel again and running a
[reindex](#freshness) publishes it once more. Each delete is recorded in the audit log.

```json
{
  "products": [
    {
      "key": "TEE-100",
      "type": "tee",
      "title": { "en": "Everyday tee" },
      "categories": ["tees"],
      "attributes": { "fabric": "cotton" },
      "channels": { "web": { "price": { "amount": 1800, "currency": "USD" } } },
      "variants": [
        { "sku": "TEE-100-M", "attributes": { "size": "m" } },
        { "sku": "TEE-100-L", "attributes": { "size": "l" } }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

Reading routes need `viewer`, writes `operator` and `reindex` `admin`; every write is in the
[audit log](/guides/team-and-roles/#audit-log) under **Catalog**.

## Feeds

For a whole file, send it as the request body of
`POST /api/v1/catalog/feeds?format=csv|ndjson&name=<label>` (`format` is one of
<Values of="feed-formats" />). The feed is read as it arrives, so its size isn't limited by a request
body. If the body stops arriving for 30 seconds, the answer is `408` and nothing is stored: send the
file again. The answer is `202 { "feed": { "id", "state", … } }`; poll `GET /api/v1/catalog/feeds/:id`
until `state` is `done` or `failed`. States: <Values of="feed-states" />. A feed reports `rows`,
`created`, `updated`, `unchanged` and `rejected` counts and, for the first 100 rejected rows,
`errors` with the `row` number, the product `key` when known and the `message`. When the whole feed
fails (for example, a CSV without `key` and `sku` columns, or text that isn't UTF-8), `failure` says why. `GET /api/v1/catalog/feeds` lists recent feeds.

To stop a feed that hasn't finished, an operator sends `POST /api/v1/catalog/feeds/:id/cancel`. The
answer is the feed, now `cancelled`, with `cancelledBy`. No more of the file is read, but the parts
already handed to the catalog still apply, so a cancelled feed may have written some products; its
counts say how many. A feed that is already `done`, `failed` or `cancelled` is left as it is, and
cancelling a `done` or `failed` one answers `409`.

The file must be UTF-8. **NDJSON** is one product per line, in the same shape as the products API,
and can express everything. **CSV** has one row per variant, a header row and RFC 4180 quoting;
all rows of a product must be contiguous (a key that appears again later replaces the earlier rows),
and the product's own columns are read from its first row. The header must have `key` and `sku`.
Row numbers in errors count the header as row 1.

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `key`, `type`, `status`, `brand` | Product fields; `type` may be `code@version` |
| `categories`, `media` | `\|`-separated lists of category codes and image URLs |
| `title:<locale>`, `slug:<locale>`, `description:<locale>` | Localized text, one column per locale |
| `price.<channel>`, `compareAt.<channel>` | Decimal prices in the channel's currency (`18.00`) |
| `visible.<channel>` | `true` or `false`; a channel with a price column is visible unless this says otherwise |
| `sku`, `gtin`, `mpn`, `variantPrice.<channel>` | Variant fields; `variantPrice` overrides the product price |
| `weightKg`, `lengthCm`, `widthCm`, `heightCm` | A variant's per-unit shipping weight and size; the three size columns are given together or not at all |
| `attr.<code>`, `attr.<code>:<locale>` | An attribute, product- or variant-scoped as its type says; the `:<locale>` form is for localized text |

Attribute cells are read by kind: `|` separates `multi_enum` values, money is `12.34 USD`, a measure
is its number, booleans are `true` or `false`. A bad cell rejects that product, with the row in `errors`.

```csv
key,type,categories,title:en,price.web,sku,attr.size,attr.fabric
TEE-100,tee,tees,Everyday tee,18.00,TEE-100-M,m,cotton
TEE-100,,,,,TEE-100-L,l,
```

## Editing content

Feeds own a product's data, but its content can be fixed in Order Loop without the next import
undoing it. The editable fields are <Values of="editable-fields" />: the title, description and slug
per locale, a product's own localized text attributes, an image's alt text, and the status. Variants,
prices, channels, categories and images stay with the feed.

**The Catalog page** (in the sidebar, under Reference) is this board for people. Gaps groups each product once:
conflicts first, then rejected rows, missing required attributes and missing text. **Draft these**
on the missing text has the assistant draft the products shown, one by one; **Accept** on a drafted
card stages its drafts; **Publish** heads Staged and publishes the ticked products. These go through
the [assistant](/guides/assistant/#edits-from-a-page). Staging, accepting and dropping run at once.
**Publish** asks on the assistant's confirm card, which lists each product and the fields going
live. If something is staged after the card appears, the confirm is refused and nothing is
published. A card opens the
product's sheet: each field in each locale side by side, marked as from the feed, edited here, a
draft, staged, a conflict or missing, opened on the field that put it on the board. Attributes read
by the label their product type gives them (else their code in words, with the code beside it), and
a refused row's issues read as sentences ("Needs at least one variant"). On a phone or an
upright iPad you can review and accept or drop drafts; editing and publishing need a larger screen.
With a keyboard, J and K move between cards, Enter opens one, D drafts it, A accepts its drafts, X
ticks it and ⌘Enter (Ctrl+Enter) publishes what is ticked. Viewers can look; operators make changes.

The same board is in the REST API:

**The board.** `GET /api/v1/catalog/board` counts products in each column (<Values of="board-columns" />)
and the gaps of each kind (<Values of="gap-kinds" />), and returns the latest finished feed and one
still running. A product moves right as it is worked on: it is on **staged** while it has edits
waiting to publish, else **drafted** while the assistant has drafts for it to review, else
**published** for a day after its edits went live, else **gaps** while something is missing (a title
in a locale of a channel it is listed in; a description, text attribute or alt text it has in some
locales but not that one; a feed row the catalog refused for its key; or a conflict, below). Its card
still counts the text it lacks, and a product published with text still missing goes back to **gaps**
when its day is up. One that needs a person (a conflict, a refused row or a missing required
attribute) goes to **gaps** at once. `GET /api/v1/catalog/board/:column` lists a column
in key order, 50 at a time by default (`limit` up to 100), optionally only products with one `kind` of
gap; pass the answer's `cursor` for the next page. The board also counts the products on **gaps** by
group (`gapGroups`: conflicts, then rejected rows, missing required, missing text; each product once).
Each card names the locales it lacks text in (`locales`), counts its gaps by kind, its staged edits
and drafts, and says why its edits didn't publish last time, if they didn't.

`GET /api/v1/catalog/products/:key/sheet` returns everything about one product: as it is live and as
the last feed sent it, its edits, staged edits, drafts, gaps, the last refused feed row and its last
50 published changes.

**Staging.** `POST /api/v1/catalog/edits` takes `{ "edits": [...] }`, up to 500, each naming the
product `key`, the field (`ref`: `field`, `locale`, and `code` for an attribute or `src` for an image)
and either `"op": "set"` with a `value` (`null` removes the feed's value in that locale) or
`"op": "clear"` to show the feed's value again. Each is checked against the product and answered
`ok`, or with `issues`. Nothing changes until it is published; a later edit to the same field
replaces the earlier one. `POST /api/v1/catalog/edits/discard` with `{ "items": [{ "key", "ref"? }] }`
drops one field's staged edit, or all of a product's.

```json
{
  "edits": [
    { "key": "TEE-100", "ref": { "field": "description", "locale": "fr" }, "op": "set", "value": "Un t-shirt en coton." },
    { "key": "TEE-100", "ref": { "field": "attribute", "code": "care", "locale": "fr" }, "op": "set", "value": "Lavage à froid" }
  ]
}
```

**Publishing.** `POST /api/v1/catalog/edits/publish` with `{ "keys": [...], "requestId"?, "urgent"? }`
publishes up to 500 products' staged edits, each product's all or none, and answers each `published`
(with its new version), `failed` (with why; its edits stay staged) or `unchanged` (nothing was
staged). A product is refused when a value it changes was changed by someone else since it was
staged (stage it again against what is there now), when an edit no longer fits, or when a slug it sets
belongs to another product. Send the same `requestId` again, after a timeout for example, to get the
first answer without publishing twice; the answer includes the one used.

**Edits and feeds.** A published edit stays over later imports and API writes of the product. Each
edit remembers the feed's value when it was published; when a later import changes that value, the
edit stays live and the product goes back on **gaps** with a `conflict`, until someone keeps the
edit (stage the same text again), takes the feed's value (`clear`) or writes new text.

**Drafts.** The assistant drafts a product's missing text: `POST /api/v1/catalog/products/:key/draft`
writes, from what the product says in its other locales, each title, description, text attribute
and image alt text the product lacks in a locale of a channel it is listed in, and saves them as
drafts. It skips a gap that already has a draft or a staged edit waiting, and writes up to 20 at a
time, titles and descriptions first; `more` in the answer counts the gaps left for another request.
Send `{ "fields": [{ "field", "locale", ... }] }` to draft those fields instead, drafts waiting or
not, and `instruction` for your own guidance (tone, length, terms to keep). The answer lists what was
`drafted` and what was `refused` and why (a field that can't be drafted, such as `status` or one
without a locale, is refused there); nothing changes on the product. It answers `422` when the
product has no gaps left to draft, your [assistant allowance](/guides/assistant/#daily-allowance) for
the day is spent, or the model couldn't answer. The [assistant](/guides/assistant/#product-content) and MCP
clients draft the same way with `draft_product_content`. Drafts written elsewhere are saved with
`POST /api/v1/catalog/drafts` (`{ "drafts": [{ "key", "ref", "value", "model"? }] }`, up to 500) and
never change the product. A draft is accepted by staging it with `"source": "assistant"`, so the
published change records where it came from; staging any edit to the field uses up its draft. The
sheet marks a draft **stale** once the field has changed since it was drafted.
`POST /api/v1/catalog/drafts/discard` drops drafts. Status is never drafted.

Reading the board and sheets needs `viewer`; staging, publishing and drafts need `operator`. Products
stored before the gap index existed are counted on their own, shortly after the catalog is updated;
until then the board's `indexBuilt` is `false`. `POST /api/v1/catalog/gaps/rebuild` (`admin`)
recounts every product now, if you'd rather not wait.

## Reading from the storefront

Shoppers' reads don't go through `/api/v1`. The catalog is served read-only and without credentials
at `https://catalog.<your domain>/v1/<channel>/<locale>/…` (`GET` and `HEAD`, open CORS). Other
methods answer `405`; a missing product answers `404 { "error" }`. Prices are the channel's currency
in minor units, and text is already in the requested locale (falling back to the channel's default).

| Route | Answers |
|---|---|
| `products/<key>` | The product: `key`, `type`, `version`, `title`, `slug`, `description`, `brand`, `categories`, `primaryCategory`, `media`, `attributes`, `variants`, `price`, `compareAt`, `priceRange`, `extra`. Each variant has `sku`, `options` (each axis as `{ code, label }`), `attributes`, `price` and `media`; enum values are `{ code, label }` |
| `skus/<sku>`, `slugs/<slug>` | The same document, found by a variant SKU or by the product's slug in that locale |
| `products?keys=a,b,c` | `{ "products": [card or null, …] }` for up to 100 keys, in the order asked; `null` for one that isn't published |
| `categories` | `{ "categories": [{ code, parent, name, slug, position }] }`, the whole tree, flat |
| `categories/<code>/products` | A page of the category's listing, below |
| `search?q=<words>` | Keyword search, best match first |

A **card** is what lists show: `key`, `version`, `title`, `slug`, `brand`, `image`, `price`, `compareAt`,
`priceRange` (when variants differ in price), `variants` (the count) and the attributes flagged `card`.

A category listing takes:

| Parameter | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `f.<attr>=a,b` | Products with any of these values of the attribute; different attributes all have to match. `brand` is always available |
| `r.<attr>=lo..hi` | An inclusive numeric range; either end may be empty (`r.price=1000..`). `price` is always available |
| `sort` | `newest` (default), or `<attr>_asc` or `<attr>_desc` for `price` and the `sortable` attributes. An unsupported sort answers `400` with the `sorts` that exist |
| `cursor`, `limit` | Page with the previous answer's `next` as `cursor`, passed back exactly as it came: it is an opaque token (a malformed one answers `400`), and pages followed this way never skip or repeat a product. `limit` is 1 to 100 (24 by default) |

The answer is `{ total, next, sorts, products, facets, ranges }`. `next` is a string, `null` on the
last page. `newest` orders by creation time to the second; products created in the same second keep
one fixed order between them.
`facets` maps each filterable attribute to `{ label, values: [{ value, label, count }] }` (at most 50
values, most products first); each attribute's counts ignore its own filter, so choosing a brand still
shows how many products every other brand has. `ranges` gives `min`, `max` and, where the attribute
declares `buckets`, counts per bucket.

A category page holds up to about 1.5 million products (fewer when products have many filterable
or sortable attributes). A larger category answers `503` with `{ "error": "listing too large" }`:
split it into subcategories, or find its products with search.

Search takes `q`, `cursor` and `limit` and answers `{ total, next, products }` (a
`partial: true` marks an answer that couldn't reach every part of the index; retry shortly). Terms
are all required and the last also matches as a prefix, so `blue cott` finds a blue cotton tee. Accents
are ignored. Its `cursor` is a number, the previous answer's `next` (an offset below 1,000), so it
pages up to 1,000 results. On the first page, a query that is a
SKU puts the product with that SKU first.

## Freshness

Documents and listings are cached. A change appears within about a minute: published documents are
fresh for 60 seconds, then revalidated with their `ETag` (an unchanged one costs a `304`; listing
pages and batches carry an `ETag` too), and a category's listing index is rebuilt at most every 15
seconds (a very large category, past 300,000 products, waits a little longer, up to 5 minutes),
however many products changed. Search and listings pick up a product once it is published, which
follows its write by moments.

Set `urgent: true` on a write or a delete for a takedown or a price correction: the cached copies of
those products are purged as soon as they are republished. Purges are rationed, so use it
sparingly; the rest of a feed shouldn't be urgent. When many urgent changes queue up, they are
purged in turn at the rate Cloudflare allows (about 500 products a minute). A purge clears the product's document at once; the card in listing pages, search results
and batches can still show the old version for up to about a minute more, while the card store
catches up around the world.

A change to a channel's currency or locales, a category that moved, or an improvement to how documents are
rendered reaches published products only on a reindex. `POST /api/v1/catalog/reindex` (`admin`) republishes
every product and answers `202 { "queued" }`.

## Orders

Order Loop's orders price and name lines from its own products. The catalog keeps that table current
for the channel the workspace's administrator has chosen for orders (`web` by default), when the
channel is in the base currency (`USD`): each active variant with a price in that channel, its own or its
product's, becomes a product with the variant's SKU, a list price in dollars, and a name made of the
product title and the variant's option labels, such as `Everyday tee (M)`, and the variant's shipping
weight and size when it has them. A SKU that already exists keeps its stock, cost and other settings
and gets the new name, price, weight and size (a variant without a weight or size clears the
product's, so shipping rated by weight stops using it); a new one starts with no
stock and zero cost, which stay managed in Order Loop.

A change applies only if it is newer than the last one the catalog wrote for that SKU. A product that
is archived, hidden in the channel or deleted keeps its rows, since orders may still name its SKUs.
Variants without a price in that channel are not written.
