A cart is a list of lines you and your team put together before it becomes an order: a quarterly
restock, a quote a customer asked for, a large B2B order three people need to agree on. Everyone
who has a cart open sees each change as it happens, who else is there, and the cart's chat.

Open **Carts** from the main navigation.

## Opening a cart

Operators open a cart with **New cart**, giving a **title**, the **flow** it will run on, and
optionally a **customer**; the new cart opens once it's made. Only published flows that price orders (they have a
[Price lines](/reference/nodes/#commerce.price) step) are offered.

The flow and the customer decide the price: a cart for a customer with contract prices shows
those prices, exactly as the order will charge them.

## Lines and prices

A line is a SKU and a quantity. Nobody types a price: after every change the cart is priced
again by its flow's own pricing steps (validate, price, shipping rate, tax and policy), the same
way an order on that flow is priced. Until the new price is in, the cart shows **Pricing…**.

- **Find a product** searches by name or SKU as you type, among the products the cart's flow
  can price, and lists up to 20 at a time (add words to narrow it). Each result shows what the
  flow charges for one, for the cart's customer, before promotions, shipping and tax; a flow that
  prices at cost shows a buyer no prices. Set a quantity and **Add**. **Or add by SKU** takes a SKU
  you already know.
- Adding a SKU the cart already has adds to that line.
- Setting a quantity to 0 removes the line.
- Tick lines (or **Select all**) to change several at once: **Set quantity** gives every ticked
  line the same quantity (0 removes them) and **Remove** takes them all off, each as one change
  priced once. A quantity of 0 removes them, and the button says so. If someone removed one of the
  ticked lines meanwhile, the page says so and **Set quantity** changes none of them until you
  choose again; a line removed and added back comes back unticked.
- A cart holds at most 100 lines, and a line at most 10,000 units.
- **Promotion codes** go in the box under the price (up to five a cart). They change the price
  only if the cart's flow has an Apply promotions step; a code that doesn't apply, or a flow
  without that step, is shown under the price, and the cart can still be submitted. A cart takes
  at most ten codes tried in an hour, by anyone, so codes can't be guessed through it.
- A SKU the store doesn't sell, or a line that is short of stock, shows its problem under the line.
  A policy the cart breaks (an order minimum, say) shows under the price.

## Ship to and shipping

When a cart has goods that ship, or already has an address, it shows **Ship to**: the address
(street, city, postcode and country, with an optional name, second line and state or region)
and, once priced, the shipping options the cart's flow offers for it, each with its price and
delivery time. Saving the address or picking an option prices the cart again, so shipping and
any tax that depends on where it goes show in the price before there is an order. When the
cart's customer holds a verified [tax exemption certificate](/guides/fulfillment/#tax-exemption)
covering the address, the price charges no tax and says so under it: "Tax exempt (certificate
…)". Until you pick
an option, the flow picks one (the cheapest, unless its shipping step says otherwise), and the
price shows that one. A flow whose shipping step always picks the cheapest or fastest charges
that one whatever you pick: the cart shows the one it charged as selected, says so under the
price, and the order ships by it. A flow that prices no shipping offers no options to pick.

- If the cart's flow offers shipping options and the cart has goods that ship, it can't be
  submitted without a complete address. A cart of digital goods, or on a flow that prices no
  shipping, needs none, and neither does a cart going back to a buyer's purchasing system, whose
  purchase order says where it ships.
- An option the flow no longer offers (after the address changed, say) stays chosen and shows
  under the price as a problem, and the cart can't be submitted until you pick another.
- **Clear** removes the address and the option with it.
- The buyer of a cart opened from their own purchasing system can set both too; what goes back to
  their system is unchanged.

## Files

Attach spec sheets, quote requests or photos under **Files**: PDF, PNG, JPEG or WebP images, CSV
or text, and Excel or Word files, up to 10 MB each and 20 a cart. Operators and the cart's buyer
can add files while the cart is open; everyone who can see the cart can download them. An
external cart's buyer can download them while the cart is open or returned to their system, not
once it is submitted, cancelled or expired. Whoever
added a file, or an operator, can remove it. **Remove** asks first, since removing deletes the
file for everyone on the cart.

## How long a cart's conversation is kept

90 days after a cart is submitted, returned or canceled, its chat, files and ship-to address are
deleted, and a buyer's name is removed from it. The cart's lines, price and order (which keeps its
own copy of the address) stay. A buyer from a purchasing system who asks to have their personal
data erased loses their messages, files and ship-to address from their carts straight away (an
open one is canceled), and a customer who does loses the ship-to address from the carts your team
opened for them.

## Working on a cart together

**Here now** lists everyone with the cart open. The **chat** is for the cart's own conversation:
messages are signed with your name, and you can edit or delete only your own. Earlier messages
load a page at a time. While someone writes a message, the chat says so under its messages
("Ann is typing…") until they send it or stop for a few seconds. On a cart a customer opened from
their purchasing system, their buyer sees when staff are typing and staff see when the buyer is.

Staff read and answer a cart's chat in the [assistant](/guides/assistant/) panel. On the cart's
page, **Open the thread** switches the panel to **Thread**, the cart's chat beside the assistant,
and **Assistant** switches back. **Draft a reply** has the assistant draft the next message from
the chat's latest messages. The draft fills the message box, marked as the assistant's until you change it, and nothing is sent
until you send it. A draft keeps a link only if someone already posted it in the chat.
The buyer of a cart opened from their purchasing system chats on their own page, as before.

| Role | Can |
|---|---|
| Viewer and Approver | Open carts, see their lines and price, and chat |
| Operator and Admin | Also open carts, change lines and the ship-to, lock and unlock, submit and cancel |

Signing out, being disabled or losing access closes the cart on any page you still have open
within about 30 seconds.

## Locking, submitting and cancelling

**Lock** stops anyone changing lines while you check the cart; **Unlock** lets them
again. A locked cart can still be submitted.

**Submit as order** starts an order on the cart's flow with the cart's lines, customer, ship-to
address and the shipping option its price charged, and the cart then links to it. The order is priced again by its flow and goes through
whatever the flow does next, approvals included: a cart is not approved on its own. A cart can be
submitted only once its price is current and shows no problems, and only once: after that, and
after **Cancel cart**, nothing changes it. On a staff cart, **Submit as order** and **Cancel cart**
go through the [assistant](/guides/assistant/#edits-from-a-page)'s confirm card; an external cart's
cancel asks on the page.

Orders started from a cart have the **Manual** channel, and their external id is the cart's id.

Opening, submitting, returning, cancelling and expiring a cart each send a signed
[outbound webhook](/guides/outbound-webhooks/#cart-events) (`cart.created`, `cart.submitted` and so
on) to the endpoints subscribed to it. Changing a cart's lines sends none.

## From the API, MCP and the assistant

Carts your team opens can also be built without the page: through the
[REST API](/reference/rest-api/#carts), from an MCP client such as Claude
([MCP server](/reference/mcp/#carts)), or by asking the [assistant](/guides/assistant/#carts).
Each change is made as the person or API token asking, with the same roles as the page: viewers
read, operators open, change, submit and cancel. Everyone with the cart open sees the change as
it lands. Submitting from MCP or the assistant asks you to confirm first, and submits only the cart as you saw it: a change in between refuses the submit. None of these read or
post to the cart's chat or files, and none reach a cart a customer opened from their own
purchasing system.

## Carts your customers open from their own systems

Some customers buy from inside their own purchasing system. An integration installed on your
workspace ([punchout](/guides/punchout/), say) opens a cart for their buyer and sends them to it, on a page outside
the app that works only for that buyer and that cart, and can be shown inside their system.
The buyer adds and changes lines and chats; staff see the same cart on the Carts page, where
they can help in the chat. When the buyer is done, **Return cart to your system** sends the
priced lines back to their purchasing system, which raises the purchase order from them, and
the cart reads **Returned to the buyer**. A cart the buyer never sends back closes on its own
when the session expires. The integration may keep the session going while the buyer works on the
cart (punchout's idle timeout), never longer than 24 hours from when it opened; the cart page
shows when it ends.

Inside the purchasing system's frame, the cart keeps its buyer signed in with a cookie kept for
that system's site alone (a partitioned third-party cookie). A browser that blocks third-party
cookies outright, as Safari does by default, doesn't keep it, so there the buyer can't use the
cart in a frame; the purchasing system has to open it in its own window or tab.
